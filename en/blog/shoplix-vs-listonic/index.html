Shoplix vs Listonic: which one fits your household?

Short version: Listonic is the more polished solo assistant — top-tier autocomplete, automatic aisle sorting, ~40 languages, a decade of refinement. Shoplix is the better shared list — item claiming, per-member spending, an event mode, offline support, and a free tier with no ads or sign-up. The right pick depends on whether you shop alone or as a team.

Where Listonic wins

Listonic has spent over a decade polishing the individual shopping experience, and it shows:

  • Smart suggestions. Its autocomplete predicts products remarkably well and files them into the right category automatically — still the benchmark in this category.
  • Aisle sorting. Lists rearrange themselves to match a typical store route, not just category sections.
  • Language reach. Around 40 interface languages to Shoplix's 11.
  • Extras. A price list with a budget planner and a pantry-check feature, even in the free tier.

If your list is written by one person and shopping is mostly a solo run, Listonic is a genuinely excellent tool — the banner and interstitial ads in the free version are its main annoyance, and Premium removes them.

Where Shoplix wins

Shoplix was built for the moment a list stops being personal — when a household or a group of friends shops from it together:

  • Claiming. Tap an item and your avatar lands on it: everyone sees you're buying the milk. This one mechanic eliminates the "we both bought bread" problem that list-sharing alone doesn't solve.
  • Awareness of changes. Items added, bought, or deleted by others stay highlighted until you've seen them, and each list shows a counter of unseen changes.
  • Event mode. For parties and BBQs: bought items remain visible to everyone, adding an already-bought duplicate triggers a warning, and spending is tallied per participant — so settling up afterwards takes seconds.
  • Budget analytics. Spending by member, by category, and by product, with a per-item price history normalized per kg/L/piece. Listonic tracks item prices and list totals; Shoplix answers "where did the money actually go this month, and who spent it?"
  • Voice that parses. Both apps take dictation, but Shoplix splits one phrase into separate items with quantities and units ("milk, bread, two kilos of apples") and learns your own product names for better recognition.
  • Works offline. The whole app works with no connection and syncs when you're back online.
  • Zero friction. The free tier has no ads and needs no account. Inviting a family member means sending a link — it opens the app straight on the join screen, or routes them through the store install first.

What Shoplix's Premium changes

To keep this honest in both directions: Shoplix isn't 100% free either. The free tier covers everything above with no ads, plus 20 templates, 20 recipes, and a monthly quota of recipe imports and barcode lookups. The optional Premium ("Shoplix Unlimited") removes those caps and adds animated themes. Claiming, sync, statistics, voice, and offline mode are never paywalled.

The bottom line

Shop alone and want aisle sorting with the best suggestions → Listonic. Shop as a household or group → Shoplix. If you're unsure, the cost of trying both is one afternoon — both are free to start, and Shoplix doesn't even ask for an email.

Frequently asked questions

Is Shoplix or Listonic better for families?

For families where several people shop, Shoplix fits better: item claiming shows who is buying what, spending is tracked per member, and the free tier has no ads or sign-up. Listonic fits better if you mostly shop alone and want automatic aisle sorting with the most polished product suggestions.

Is Shoplix really free without ads?

Yes. Shoplix's free tier shows no advertising, requires no account, and includes shared lists, claiming, voice input, statistics, and offline mode. An optional Premium unlocks unlimited templates and recipes (the free tier includes 20 of each), unlimited barcode lookups, and animated themes. Listonic's free version is ad-supported; its paid Premium (a subscription or a lifetime purchase) removes ads.

Do Shoplix and Listonic both have voice input?

Yes, both. Listonic's voice input is long-refined and well regarded. Shoplix's dictation splits a whole phrase into separate items and parses quantities and units ("milk, bread, two kilos of apples"), works in every language the app supports, and learns your own product names for better recognition.

Can I use Shoplix or Listonic in a web browser?

Both offer a web version alongside their mobile apps. Shoplix's web app at app.shoplix.app works without registration; Listonic's web app requires an account.
