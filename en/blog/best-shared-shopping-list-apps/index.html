The 6 best shared shopping list apps in 2026

The best shared shopping list app in 2026 depends on what your household needs: Shoplix for real-time lists with item claiming and budget stats, AnyList for meal planning, Bring! for visual browsing, Listonic for smart suggestions and aisle sorting, OurGroceries for simplicity with voice assistants, and Google Keep if you want no new apps at all.

Full disclosure: we make Shoplix, one of the apps in this list. To keep the comparison useful we've stuck to verifiable facts about every app, listed real weaknesses of our own product, and made clear which alternative is the better pick for each situation.

How we picked

A shared shopping list app has one job: when several people shop for one household, nothing gets forgotten and nothing gets bought twice. We compared each app on the five things that decide whether that actually happens:

  • Real-time sync — do changes show up on everyone's phone in seconds, not minutes?
  • Coordination features — can you see who is buying what (claiming, assignments, activity)?
  • Friction — how fast can a non-technical family member start using it? Sign-up walls and ads count against.
  • Beyond the list — budgets, price history, recipes, voice input, barcode scanning.
  • Price — what the free tier really includes, and what's paywalled.

1. Shoplix

Best for households & events

iOS · Android · Web — free core with no ads or sign-up; optional Premium

Pick Shoplix if several people genuinely shop for one household or event and you want to see, in real time, who is buying what — without ads or creating an account.

Shoplix is built around one problem the other apps mostly ignore: duplicate purchases. Every item on a shared list has a claim slot — tap it and your avatar appears on the item, so nobody else grabs the same milk on the way home. Changes sync to every phone in seconds, a counter on each list shows what changed since you last looked, and the app keeps working without a connection, syncing when you're back online.

It's also the only app in this list with per-member budget analytics in the free tier: spending by member, by category, and by product, with a per-item price history. Voice input understands whole phrases ("milk, bread, two kilos of apples") and a barcode scanner recognizes products automatically. Two list modes (Household and Event) change the behavior — Event mode keeps bought items visible to everyone, warns about duplicates, and tallies spending per participant, which makes it the strongest pick for birthdays, BBQs, and potlucks.

Pros

  • Free core with no ads and no sign-up wall
  • Item claiming with avatars — see who's buying what
  • Spending stats by member, category, and product with price history
  • Voice input that parses several items with quantities per phrase
  • Barcode scanner with automatic product recognition
  • Works offline; interactive home-screen widgets; recipe import from a link or text

Cons

  • Newer app — smaller community than veterans like AnyList
  • No meal-planning calendar, no watch app, no Siri/Alexa integration
  • Power features need Premium: unlimited templates and recipes (free tier caps both at 20) and unlimited barcode lookups

2. AnyList

Best for meal planning

iOS · Android · Apple Watch · Web (paid) — free core; paid Complete subscription

Pick AnyList if your shopping list is really an extension of a weekly meal plan and you're happy to pay for the full experience.

AnyList is the long-standing favorite for households that cook seriously, and its free tier is genuinely generous: shared lists with fast sync, voice commands, autocomplete, item photos and prices — without ads. The recipe box and recipe-to-list flow are the most mature in the category.

The best parts live in the paid Complete tier (billed yearly, with a household option): web and desktop access, unlimited recipe import from websites, the meal-planning calendar, budget totals with multi-store price comparison, and Apple Watch support. And there's no concept of claiming an item, so coordinating who buys what still happens in the family chat.

Pros

  • Best-in-class recipes and meal planning
  • Generous ad-free free tier with voice and prices
  • Mature and reliable; great Apple ecosystem support

Cons

  • Web access and unlimited recipe import require the subscription
  • No item claiming or member spending stats
  • Feels heavyweight for a simple shared list

3. Bring!

Best visual experience

iOS · Android · Web — free with sponsored content; paid Premium

Pick Bring! if you shop visually and enjoy browsing — its tile-based lists with product images are the nicest to look at in the category.

Bring! replaces the classic checklist with a grid of product tiles, which many households find faster to scan in the store. It has a large product catalog, seasonal recipe inspiration, solid shared-list basics with fast sync, and topped NerdWallet's 2026 grocery-app roundup.

The free version includes sponsored recipes and offers (Premium removes ads and adds themes), and like most competitors it can't tell you who is buying an item or what your household actually spends.

Pros

  • Beautiful, fast tile-based interface
  • Recipe and inspiration content built in
  • Reliable sharing across platforms

Cons

  • Sponsored content in the free tier
  • No claiming, budgets, or price history
  • Tiles get unwieldy for very long lists

4. Listonic

Best smart suggestions

iOS · Android · Web — free with ads; paid Premium (subscription or lifetime)

Pick Listonic if you want the most polished autocomplete with automatic aisle sorting and the widest language support.

Listonic has been refining grocery lists for over a decade. Its autocomplete is genuinely smart, voice input works well, lists sort themselves by store aisle, and there's a price list and budget planner plus a pantry-check feature. It's localized into roughly 40 languages and has a capable web app.

The free version shows banner and interstitial ads (Premium, available as a subscription or a lifetime purchase, removes them), and collaboration stays basic: you can share a list, but there's no claiming, no per-member spending, and no event-style workflow. We've written a detailed Shoplix vs Listonic comparison if you're choosing between the two.

Pros

  • Excellent autocomplete with automatic aisle sorting
  • Price list and budget planner; pantry check
  • Long track record, ~40 languages

Cons

  • Banner and interstitial ads in the free version
  • No claiming or member-level stats
  • Sharing is list-by-list, no household concept

5. OurGroceries

Simplest option

iOS · Android · Web · Apple Watch · Wear OS — free with ads; paid ad removal (incl. one-time option)

Pick OurGroceries if you want a dependable shared list that any family member can learn in thirty seconds — and that answers to your smart speaker.

OurGroceries has dependably done one thing for years: shared lists that sync fast across every family device. It's better equipped than its plain looks suggest — barcode scanning, photo attachments, aisle sorting with region-specific categories, watch apps on both platforms, and voice adding through Alexa, Siri, or Google Assistant.

The interface shows its age, the free version has ads (removed by a paid upgrade, including a one-time purchase for the whole household), and there's no coordination layer — no claiming, no budgets, no price tracking.

Pros

  • Extremely simple — zero learning curve
  • Alexa, Siri, and Google Assistant voice adding
  • Barcode scanning; Apple Watch and Wear OS apps; one-time lifetime upgrade available

Cons

  • Dated interface, ads in free version
  • No claiming or member spending stats
  • No price history or budget analytics

6. Google Keep

Best non-app option

iOS · Android · Web — completely free

Pick Google Keep if your household already lives in Google apps and refuses to install anything new.

Keep isn't a shopping app — it's a notes app with shareable checklists. But it's free, everyone already has it, it takes voice input through Google Assistant, and a shared checklist note honestly covers a two-person household with modest needs.

You'll feel the limits quickly: no categories or aisle sorting, no quantities or prices, no claiming, and checked items just pile up at the bottom. It's the baseline the dedicated apps are competing against.

Pros

  • Free, no new app to install
  • Familiar to everyone; Assistant voice input
  • Fine for very simple needs

Cons

  • No grocery features at all
  • Long lists become unmanageable
  • No structure: categories, quantities, prices

How to choose: quick scenarios

  • Family where several people shop → Shoplix. Claiming plus real-time sync is the only reliable fix for duplicate purchases.
  • You meal-plan every week → AnyList, and accept the subscription.
  • You want the prettiest app → Bring!.
  • You want automatic aisle sorting and top-tier autocomplete → Listonic.
  • You add items through Alexa or a smartwatch → OurGroceries.
  • Grandparents are on the list too → OurGroceries or Shoplix (no sign-up helps here).
  • Organizing a party or BBQ → Shoplix's Event mode: bought items stay visible, duplicates trigger a warning, and everyone's spending is tallied per person.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best free shared shopping list app?

Shoplix has the strongest free tier in 2026: real-time shared lists, item claiming, voice input, barcode scanning, budget statistics, and recipe import — with no ads and no sign-up, on iOS, Android, and the web. An optional Premium adds unlimited templates, recipes, and barcode lookups. Google Keep is also completely free, but it's a notes app without grocery-specific features.

Can two people edit the same shopping list at the same time?

Yes. Dedicated shared list apps such as Shoplix, AnyList, Bring!, Listonic, and OurGroceries sync each change to every participant's phone within seconds, so several people can add and check off items simultaneously without overwriting each other.

Which shared shopping list app works on both iPhone and Android?

All six apps in this guide work on iPhone and Android. Shoplix, Listonic, OurGroceries, and Google Keep also offer a web version; AnyList's web and desktop access is part of its paid Complete tier.

How do shared shopping lists prevent buying the same item twice?

The most reliable mechanism is item claiming: a shopper marks an item as theirs and everyone else sees it instantly. Shoplix is built around this — each item shows the avatar of the person who claimed it, and its Event mode additionally warns when someone adds an item that's already bought.
